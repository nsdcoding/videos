📌 Quick summary: a positive result from a good test (catches 9 in 10, false alarm 1 in 10) means only about an 8% chance when the condition affects 1 in 100 people. Picture 1,000 people: 9 real positives vs 99 false alarms (2:28). The rarer the thing, the more the false alarms win (8:17).

Worth adding:
1️⃣ The numbers are illustrative, chosen so the counting comes out in whole people. Real tests have their own hit and false-alarm rates, and your own base rate depends on age, symptoms and history. Ask your doctor what YOUR result means.
2️⃣ Multiplying the odds test after test assumes each test's mistakes are independent of the last. A repeat of the same test on the same sample can repeat the same false alarm (7:57).
3️⃣ The study of doctors (1:50) is Gigerenzer et al. (2007): in a screening puzzle with numbers like ours, about 21% of the gynaecologists picked the right answer (about 1 in 10) and 47% picked "9 in 10".
